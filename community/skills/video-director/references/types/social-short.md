# Social short

A vertical (9:16) or square video of 6-30 s for TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts or a LinkedIn feed, usually watched muted while scrolling. The first frame has to stop the thumb, and the message has to land in text and picture alone.

## Ask in the brief

- Platforms and formats (9:16, 1:1, 4:5), and the cutdowns needed (6, 15, 30 s).
- Is it cut from a longer 16:9 film, or made for vertical from the start?
- Loop, a short tag, or no end card?

## Structure

Hook → situation → escalation → payoff → tag (or loop). In proportion:
- **Hook**: frame 0 and the first moment. The strangest or most colorful picture the film has, with the hook line already in place.
- **Situation**: who or what, in one shot. Something visibly changes within the first second or two.
- **Escalation**: a few beats of about 1-4 s, the same structure each beat with one thing varying (place, object, speaker). The line stays put.
- **Payoff**: the product, the result or the quiet picture, held still at least 1.5 s.
- **Tag**: no end card, a loop back, or a short line or logo (about 1.5 s or less). 4 of 8 shorts studied had no end card, and none showed a web address.

A 6 s cut keeps only the hook, one situation shot, the payoff and a brief tag; a 30 s cut adds a second escalation and a pause beat.

## Rules

- **Frame 0 is a finished picture**, never a fade-in, a title card or a logo. Say the premise at once.
- **One fixed text position for the whole short**; only the content changes.
- **Text the viewer must read is at least about 3% of frame height; a hook line about 4-5%.** Use a heavier weight, and put text over a plain area, on a pill, or with an outline or shadow. Light, small type doesn't survive a phone feed.
- **Keep lines short**, and check the total time a line is on screen across beats, not per beat. At 6 s, shorten the words; never shrink the type.
- **Words arrive in the order spoken** when they arrive word by word.
- **Hard cuts or matches are the transition**; no crossfades.
- **End on the quietest picture**, held 1.5 s or longer. The one-second still hold applies to the payoff and the line, not to every beat.
- **No "the end" card on films under 15 s.** End blocks belong to longer films, not shorts.

## Safe zones (1080x1920)

| Platform | Keep text, faces, logos and product clear of | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram and Facebook Reels | top 14%, bottom 35%, 6% each side | Meta's published video ad specs |
| TikTok | the buttons, username and caption; the zone shrinks as the caption gets longer | TikTok ads help (templates, no numbers) |
| YouTube Shorts | the red area of Google's template | Google Ads help (template image) |

House default for 9:16, built on Meta's numbers: keep text, faces, logos and product between about 15% and 65% of the height, 6% from the left, with extra room on the right for the like and share column. Backgrounds and full-bleed pictures may fill the whole frame. Check against each platform's own preview before a real campaign. For 1:1 and 4:5, keep the 5% title-safe margin; no platform publishes overlay zones for these.

Set `BRAND.size = [1080, 1920]` in the frame kit; the layout check then uses this box.

## Where text sits

Near the top of the safe area (about 15-30% down) with the picture below it, or centered (about 42-60% down) when a picture fills the frame.

## Cutting one film into 6, 15 and 30 s

- Build from units (hook, situation, escalation beats, payoff, tag), not scenes.
- Cut from the end, not the start: drop escalation beats first, then the situation. Never drop the hook or the payoff.
- The line keeps the same position and type in every cut.

## Adapting a 16:9 film: rebuild, don't crop

- A 9:16 crop of a 16:9 frame keeps about a third of the picture; letterboxing shrinks the type to about half. Both fail.
- Rebuild each scene: one subject, the line in the home position, the hero below it, supporting pieces above or below rather than beside.
- Lines get less width, so rewrap and shorten.
- Sideways camera moves tend to work better as up or down moves, and docking or fanning out as columns or stacks (compare both orientations in the animated test before choosing).

## Examples seen in strong films (not rules)

- A loop made by ending on the same shot as frame 0 with the text cleared (Duolingo).
- One fixed line over a steady cut every 1.4 s (Notion, "prehistoric tools").
- The old way failing, then the product as the answer in the last two seconds (Notion, "Joyce").
- A standing label at the top over changing faces (Figma).
- A typed hook, then product alternating with footage (Apple Creator Studio).

## Direction options differ by

The hook picture and the repeated beat (same actor in new places, same line over new faces, one object in three states), not color.

## Films worth studying

Duolingo "time to do your streakity", Notion "prehistoric tools" and "Joyce", Headspace (orange), Figma "Apologies in advance", Apple Creator Studio.

## Default versus better

| Default | Better |
|---|---|
| Opening on a title card, logo or fade-in | Frame 0 is a finished, odd picture with the hook line already placed |
| A 16:9 film letterboxed or center-cropped | Every scene rebuilt vertically, line on top, hero below |
| Captions at the bottom | Text inside the safe box, clear of the platform's buttons and captions |
| The whole sentence at once | Words arriving in the order spoken, or one short line fixed while the picture changes |
| Everything in the first second to feel fast | Hook picture and line first, then one new visible thing at a time |
| A different look for each beat | The same actor or object and line; one thing varies each beat |
| A 3 s logo card | End on the payoff; loop back, or a short tag |
| Small, light type to look refined | Type at 3-5% of the height, with a pill, outline or shadow |
