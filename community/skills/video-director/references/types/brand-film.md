# Brand film

What a company stands for, told through one idea, people and places, usually with little or no product. A manifesto, an anthem or a brand story; 30-90 s. The viewer should feel the idea and remember who it came from.

## Ask in the brief

- **The idea as one sentence.** Nothing else gets designed before this exists.
- Real people and footage, or generated stand-ins? Anyone real and named (a founder, a customer) needs the client's own photo or footage.
- Voice, on-screen type, or both?
- Is the product shown at all? Half of the strong films studied show none.

## Structure (share of running time)

| Beat | Share | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Seed | first 5-14% | One specific human image, first words or voice. No logo, very few words; a quiet or wordless start is common |
| Run | to the turn | The unit repeats at a steady length (about 1-3 s): same crop, same type position, same transition, different content |
| Turn | roughly 45-80% | The first change of stance: the voice changes, a question, a color shift, or one noticeably longer hold |
| Second run | to about 85% | The unit continues; in some films it runs noticeably faster here |
| Claim | about 80-90% | The idea in one short line, held on its own; the picture settles |
| Brand | last 3-13% | Logo or tagline once, small, on the film's own surface |

## Rules

1. **Build the film from one repeated unit** that delivers the sentence: a face with a phrase, a headline with its picture, a maker in their place, a person with a spoken clause. Fix the unit's crop, type position, transition and rough length before drawing; vary only the content.
2. **Don't state the product.** Show it only as proof: brief inserts, or one short beat after the claim.
3. **The claim is said once, late and short**, with the longest hold of the film.
4. **People are the content.** Each person appears once or twice, with one visible fact that makes them specific (a turban, glasses, sparks, a tape measure) and no caption.
5. **A face either looks at the lens or is absorbed in doing something.**
6. **When faces fill the frame, lock the crop and the eye line** (for example, eyes about a third down, same size) so transitions line up and the cast reads as one set.
7. **Places have clutter, practical light and a camera angle that isn't eye level** (top-down, behind, wide, low). Real workplaces and landscapes, not offices; several distinct camera setups.
8. **Vary the scale**; don't stay at one distance for long.

## Type and footage

- **The type is the voice, and it sits over the picture in the middle band, about 40-57% down** (default 42-52%; `CAPTION(text, {mode: "mid"})`). Set it in a quiet part of the picture, and reframe the picture to make room rather than moving the type.
- Keep the type consistent: one face, one size per role, sentence case, no labels above, no tracked capitals. Short statement lines are large; quiet editorial lines about 2-4% of the height.
- Lines arrive whole, or word by word at one pace for the whole film (about 0.1-0.3 s apart, or each word with the voice or beat; `../text-treatments.md`). The complete line then holds at least the reading time in `../text-and-captions.md`.
- **Type can stay while the picture changes under it.** The words are what survives the cut.
- Names aren't written inside the picture, except a name card for one authority person, if there is one.
- Type over a face: some brand films set lines on a calm, still face between the eyes and the mouth. This loosens the rule that lines never sit on a face, so ask first; never over the eyes or mouth, never on a speaking face.

## Safe area

No text below 60% of the height: set `BRAND.clearBottom = 0.40` in the frame kit. The end logo is the exception (it may sit as low as about 76%); mark it `allow-low`.

## Rhythm and sound

- About 10-40 units, depending on length and unit size. The one-second still hold applies to the complete line and the claim, not to every unit.
- Music is usually mastered flat in volume; build with cut rate and content. Plan one dip or stop in the music, for example at the claim or the low point.
- With narration, the picture shows what is said and the cuts follow the voice; type repeats at most a few phrases.

## Transitions

One transition type for the whole film. With footage, the hard cut is that type, and what survives each cut is often the words, the eye line or the voice rather than an object. With stills, one wipe type. A very short text blur-out and a dissolve into the end card are the only soft moves.

## The logo moment

- Once, last, after the claim, in the final 3-13% of the film. Nothing before carries the logo.
- Small and roughly centered (about 44-76% down), on the film's own surface, not a new background.
- The claim line holds on its own before the logo joins; the logo then holds about 1-4 s (median about 2.7 s in the films studied).
- End on the claim in one line, then the logo alone. Add a web address only when the film asks the viewer to do something specific (a cause, a sign-up); set it small under or beside the logo and hold about 3-4 s (1 of 8 studied: Ad Council).
- The end often changes ground or color; keep the body of the film in one graded palette.

## Generated stand-ins for shot footage

- **Portraits**: generate the whole cast in one brief as a set, with one lens, one distance, one eye line, neutral expression, a plain dark ground and one grade. Crop from forehead to chin; details such as ears, teeth and hairlines are where generated faces look wrong. Ask for pores, asymmetry and flyaway hair; reject faces with matched symmetry or mismatched catchlights.
- **Keep full-frame generated faces and clips short** (about 2-3 s). Keep the motion in the type and the transition, not in the face.
- **Clips where faces are absent or small**: hands and tools, sparks, water, weather, rooms from behind, silhouettes, top-down work. Avoid speech, crowds, sports, long close-ups of precise handwork, and any text on a surface.
- **One grade and one grain over everything**, applied in the build, hides seams between generated and supplied material.
- **What reads as filmed**: a specific person in a specific place doing a specific task; clutter and imperfection; a camera position with a point of view; practical light with color. Put these in every image brief.
- **A film of stills falls into a slideshow when every still gets the same slow push.** Hold stills still and let the type and the transition move.

## Examples seen in strong films (not rules)

- One sentence split across many faces so no single face says it; the last piece lands on the longest hold (Airbnb).
- Units that shorten sharply through the last quarter before releasing into the claim (Squarespace, NYT, Airbnb).
- The music stopping for the claim (NYT).
- The person from the first shot returning under the logo (Shopify).
- Group portraits saved for the payoff (Shopify).

## Direction options differ by

The repeated unit (face and phrase, headline and picture, maker in place, spoken clause) and how the sentence is delivered across it.

## Films worth studying

Apple "Think Different" (unnamed faces, one voice, type only at the end), Airbnb "We Accept" (one sentence across many faces), The New York Times "Life Needs Truth" (type then picture, accelerating, music drops for the claim), Patagonia "Unfashionable" (words carried across a cut), Squarespace "You Should" (accelerating makers, one clean type moment), Shopify "built different" (a color arc, the first person returns under the logo).

## Default versus better

| Default | Better |
|---|---|
| A montage of smiling diverse people with a values voiceover | One repeated unit with a locked crop and eye line, each unit carrying part of one sentence |
| A tagline and logo in a corner throughout | No logo until the end; type mid-frame in the quiet band of the picture |
| A quick flat logo card | The claim on the end surface, held on its own; then the logo |
| A list of values as nouns | A sentence in a human voice, or a run of real subjects, with the claim only at the end |
| Stock b-roll (laptops, coffee, city time-lapse) | A specific person in their own place doing the task, from a different camera angle each time |
| Steady shots of one length from start to end | A steady unit with a turn, and the longest hold for the claim |
| A slow push on every still | Stills held still; the type and one transition do the moving |
