# Event, recap and data video

Three related kinds, about 60-120 s: **openers and teasers** for an event or announcement; **recaps** of something that happened (an event, a launch day, a year); and **data stories** that make numbers felt. They share one problem: many photos, clips or numbers in a row that must not feel like a slideshow.

## Ask in the brief

- **Which kind**: opener or teaser, footage recap, launch recap, year in review, or data story.
- **What real material exists.** Recaps need real footage or the client's own photos; a generated crowd or audience reads as stock. Generated media can stand in only for illustration.
- **Music**: a track with a clear beat (a motion-graphics film can lock to it), or a bed under speech.
- **The numbers and their sources** for a data story.

## Rules

1. **Put one carrier on every clip**: a typed line, a badge, a name bar, a cursor, a chapter color. The carrier is the film; the clips are what it passes over.
2. **Cut on motion**, letting action continue across the cut.
3. **Hold one shared color range** across lighting, set and title cards, or give each chapter its own color.
4. **Alternate shot types** (stage, floor, face; wide, tight, detail) so no one type dominates.
5. **Long holds (2-4 s) only for a named person or a typed line**; everything else moves on quickly. The one-second still hold applies to numbers, names and typed lines, not to each footage shot.
6. **Rhythm**: either a steady cut rate (fine for event recaps when the picture alternates), or slow then faster. In footage recaps a quarter to a half of shots run under a second, most of the rest 1-2 s.
7. **Footage recaps cut on speech and action** over a steady music bed rather than on the beat. A motion-graphics film can lock its chapter changes to blocks of the track's beats.
8. **Numbers: draw the form, then land the number, then hold it still long enough to read.** The numeral doesn't move during the hold; the carrier may.
9. **A count-up is short and then holds.** A long count stands for time passing, not a reveal.
10. **Make the hero number large with the unit small beside it.**
11. **Vary the form when the measures differ** (bars for months, a dial for time of day), and keep the carrier, a small set of flat colors and the label style the same, so variety lives in the form.
12. **Minimal chart furniture**: no axes or legends unless the axis is the point; label only the items the story is about.
13. **One statement of at most two lines before the logo.**

## Text

- Title cards centered.
- Names of real people on footage: low on the left, small, shown briefly, once per person. This is the one exception to the empty bottom band (`allow-low`).
- Hold a typed line long enough to read.

## Close

Two common options: repeat the opening card, or resolve the carrier into the logo. A still lockup holds about 1.5-3.5 s; longer over a slowly moving ground or a music tail. End on one line or the logo; never a two-sentence tagline (8 of 8 studied). Add a web address only when the film asks for something the viewer can do now: donate, read the report, stream it (3 of 8 studied: charity: water, GitHub Octoverse, Eurobank). Event recaps end on the logo or on footage.

## Examples seen in strong films (not rules)

- **Opener**: a wordless chain of objects turning into each other, about 2 s each, with one hard cut where the music changes section and a tiny two-line statement at the end (Apple event opener).
- **Footage recap**: cold open on the hall, a title card, stage and floor and faces alternating with each speaker named, then the title card again over a moving ground (Stripe Sessions).
- **Launch recap**: chapters that each open on a giant title card and a cursor-led demo, with a new chapter color each time (Figma Config).
- **Year in review**: a slow personal opening in small windows, then an accelerating full-frame montage with one typed query riding every clip (Google Year in Search).
- **Data story**: one form per number, chapters locked to the music's beat blocks (Strava "Year in Sport" by Vucko, a studio piece).
- **Data in footage**: a counter pinned to faces, changing person as it climbs, ending in a roll call of faces in one badge shape that becomes the logo (Strava "Every 19 Seconds").
- Scale shown as one against many: one bar rising out of twelve, 30 blocks beside "30".
- Highlighting by dimming everything except one item.
- A large ghost year behind a chart so the chart needs few labels.
- Small windows for personal clips, full frame for big public moments.
- A near-silent start that makes the first typed line land.

## Direction options differ by

The carrier (an object chain, a typed line, a badge, a pointer) and the rhythm (steady, slow-then-fast, beat-locked).

## Films worth studying

Apple event opener, Stripe Sessions recap, Figma Config launch recap, Google Year in Search, Strava "Year in Sport" (Vucko), Strava "Every 19 Seconds".

## Default versus better

| Default | Better |
|---|---|
| A big number counting up from zero on a card | The form builds first, the number lands, then holds still |
| A number alone | A countable or comparable object: blocks, one against many, people behind it |
| A statistic as an overlay on b-roll | The number tied to the people in the shot |
| Crowd shots under music at an even pace | Stage, floor and faces alternating, each speaker named once |
| A sequence of unrelated clips | One carrier riding every clip |
| A chart with axes and a legend | Only the items the story is about, labeled |
| Cutting footage on the beat | Footage cut on speech and action; beat-lock only motion graphics |
