# Demo or tutorial

Teaching someone to do a task in a product or process, with the UI staged, zoomed and animated rather than recorded raw. Often narrated; 60-180 s. The viewer should be able to do the task afterward. (A raw screen recording of a real product is a different job; this type rebuilds and stages the screen.)

## Ask in the brief

- Narrated or silent? Step length depends on it.
- The real product's screens or a demo account, so the taught control matches exactly.
- The finished result to show early.

## Structure

| Beat | Share | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | first 10% | Title, plus a picture of the finished result or the whole product, held; voice, if any, starts early |
| Steps | most of the film | About 3-7 steps. Each: wide view, move in, action, result held, move back out |
| Chapter break | short, optional | A card or color change that marks a change of situation |
| Payoff | the longest, slowest stretch | The result in use, bigger and slower than the steps |
| End card | last 5-10% | Logo, line, or the next step to take |

For a multi-feature tour, use chapters with the same internal rhythm, each with its own color or index word.

## Rules

- **One action per step**: one tap, one toggle, one typed phrase. With a voice a step holds about 6-12 s; without, about 3-5 s. Sub-shots inside a step can run 1.5-3 s. Group steps into chapters when there are more than about seven.
- **A step has four parts**: arrive (about a second of zoom or cut), act (the cursor or finger travels and presses), result (the screen answers right away), hold (at least a second; longer if the result needs reading).
- **A step starts and finishes in the same shot**; never cut away mid-step.
- **Start each step with the whole object in view**, then move in, act, and move back out. Use a wide view and one or two closer levels, and reuse them all film.
- **Text the viewer must read is at least 4-5% of frame height.** At full-device view the UI is a picture, not something to read.
- **Fewer callouts.** Strong tutorials use no numbered badges, arrows or boxes; they isolate the target, use one short tooltip beside it, or let the voice name it.
- **Enlarge the cursor** so it reads at phone size; it arrives and stops before it presses, and the screen answers. Several cursors in different colors only when collaboration is the topic.
- **Anchor the viewer** with something that stays: the same device, a brand-color surround, a colored chapter background, or an index word naming the current chapter. More hard cuts stay readable when the backdrop, cursor position and color hold across them.
- **Show early what the film will teach.**
- **How the product is shown is a choice.** Offer it as the first look decision (stage 5). Of 8 tutorials studied: the interface rebuilt in 3 (Apple Pay, Oura, Figma Slides), live action of hands and the device in 3 (Dyson, Nespresso, DJI), 2D illustration in 1 (Exploding Kittens), a presenter with UI inserts in 1 (OpenAI Academy). Software tasks suit a rebuilt interface; physical products suit live action. When the UI is rebuilt: real words only for the control being taught, grey bars for everything else, and the taught control matches the real product exactly.
- **Name the proof for each claim.** Each step's result already shows the task works. Where the script claims more (faster, fewer steps, it finds X), write beside the claim what on screen shows it: a counter, a before and after, the result arriving (beat method: `../visual-ideas.md`).
- **Leave the UI** when the outcome is an experience (a meditation, a workout, a conversation) and cut away for as long as it needs; also to mark a chapter or to show a person.

## Narration

- One action per sentence, about 150 words a minute.
- The camera moves slightly before the sentence that names the target; the result lands near the end of that sentence.
- Don't repeat on screen what the voice says. Without a voice, the picture carries the action: typed text, the highlighted control, one tooltip.

## Text and safe area

- Step text sits beside its target, never over it. Chapter and title cards are centered on a clear card, long enough to read.
- The bottom 17% stays clear of added text and callouts; the product UI itself and a small legal line may reach the edge (`allow-low`).

## Close

About 3-9 s: the logo, or one short line and the logo (logo holds of 1.0-9.9 s in the films studied, median 3.5 s). The real next step usually happens inside the steps (DJI shows its app's QR code mid-film). Add a web address only when the next step is on a page (2 of 8 studied); keep it small and plain.

## Examples seen in strong films (not rules)

- Dimming everything except one choice in a list for a few seconds (Apple Call Screening).
- One dark tooltip pill beside the cursor, shown briefly (Figma Slides).
- Typing shown sped up, then the finished text held briefly before the press (Google Gemini).
- The zoomed device running off the frame edge, which reads as closer rather than smaller (Apple).
- A cycling word that tracks the current chapter (Google Gemini).

## Direction options differ by

How the product is staged (a device on a clean ground, a floating window, a tilted surface), the orientation anchor, and the callout approach.

## Films worth studying

Apple Support (Call Screening, Stage Manager), Google Workspace Gemini, Headspace app tour, Figma Slides, Notion Calendar, Slack quick start.

## Default versus better

| Default | Better |
|---|---|
| A full-screen recording of the whole app, tiny text | The device or window staged on a clean ground, shown whole, then zoomed in for the detail and back |
| Red boxes, arrows and numbered badges | Isolate the target, or one short tooltip beside it |
| The full real UI with every label | In a rebuilt UI, grey bars everywhere except the control being taught |
| "Step 2" title cards | A color change or short card per chapter |
| A real-size pointer drifting at constant speed | An enlarged pointer that arrives, stops, presses once, and gets an answer |
| Staying inside the UI for the outcome | Cutting to the experience or a person |
