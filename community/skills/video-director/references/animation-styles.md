# Animation style palette

Use these as directions to consider, not presets. Pick candidates for the film's subject, audience, brand and lead medium, then make 2–3 actual still options for the same scene. Explain what each style contributes to the idea. A style may serve a whole film or a purposeful insert; follow the one-drawing-style and lead-medium rules in SKILL.md.

Start from the group that fits the lead medium: a footage-led film usually picks from **Live-action looks**, a motion-graphics film from the drawn, graphic or 3D groups. Directions may come from different groups when the brief leaves the medium open.

## How to use the examples

- **Example to study** names a well-known film, title sequence, music video or ad where the style can be seen. Use it to look the style up, or as a reference film when the user chose reference videos (`reference-films.md`). Check access before relying on it; record only what you actually inspected.
- Rows marked **Showcase 0:00** come from [Jesse Eisenbart's 20-style showcase](https://www.tiktok.com/@jesseeisenbart/video/7697657520793324818); the time locates the example. Those descriptions come from sampled stills, not verified motion recipes.
- The descriptions say what the style looks like, not how a source moved. Never infer movement, timing or construction from a still or a description. Design original motion with the bundled motion guidance; to reproduce a specific source movement, inspect that movement first, or offer original motion without claiming source fidelity.
- Selection cues are suggestions, not claims made by any source. Adapt the visual principle to the brief rather than copying the example's look.

## Fit and constraints

Apply the house rules, including no gradient text or logo glow, unless the user explicitly overrides them. For Word art, use dimensional or arched lettering in permitted solid colors. Light-painting strokes and Neon grid lines are illustrative objects; they do not authorize glowing logos or glowing text. Aurora scenery does not authorize a blended designed background: use permitted brand treatment or show the natural scene as media. Where a type file explicitly offers an exception, follow its approval requirement. A note you author in the storyboard alone grants no exception. An explicit user instruction can override a skill rule; record that instruction and preserve its scope without implying broader permission.

Live-action looks depend on footage: supplied clips or generated stills and clips made under `media.md`, which keeps text, UI and logos out of generated media. Stop-motion, clay and paper looks made with generated media must hold one consistent look across every shot; check that before choosing them. Retro interfaces are illustrative unless they match the real product. Keep text readable and test op-art, dither, glitch, flicker-prone and fast-changing styles for legibility in the actual render. Never name a direction after a film, director or company; name the style. Define moves with the bundled motion library and motion spec after still approval.
